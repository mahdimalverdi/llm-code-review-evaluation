# ARXIV-0002 extraction packet

- Title: TrustChain-Review: A Risk-Adaptive Blockchain and Game-Theoretic Framework for Trustworthy AI-Assisted Code Review
- arXiv: 2607.27310v1
- Authors: Mohammad Naserameri
- Full text: `data/search/provisional-extraction/supporting/ARXIV-0002.txt`
- Screening tier: supporting

## Required extraction

1. Purpose: proposes an evidence, incentive, and risk-adaptive governance framework for trustworthy AI-assisted review.
2. System/context: blockchain evidence layer, three-player game model, and risk-triggered governance; calibrated with the Diff Quality Estimation dataset.
3. Study design: six governance configurations, 30 independent simulation runs; evaluator is simulation-based rather than human annotation.
4. Problematic risks: unreliable reviews, superficial approvals, malicious feedback, and unverifiable reputation; reported in the abstract/introduction.
5. Metrics/results: reputation accuracy, trust convergence, malicious- and superficial-review detection, platform utility, governance cost, and cost-efficiency. Full evidence configuration performs best on trust/detection but costs more; risk-adaptive governance improves cost-efficiency.
6. Trade-off: stronger evidence and governance improve trust and detection at higher operational cost; selective governance trades some detection performance for lower cost.
7. Limitations: controlled simulation and calibration dataset limit direct evidence about developer usefulness, comment actionability, and real review workflow. Quality score: provisional, medium.
8. Synthesis claim: this is supporting evidence for governance cost, risk-sensitive escalation, provenance, and evaluator validity; it is not direct evidence of generated-comment quality.
