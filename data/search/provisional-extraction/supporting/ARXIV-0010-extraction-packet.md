
# ARXIV-0010 extraction packet

- Title: From Human-Centric to Agentic Code Review: The Impact of Different Generations of Generative AI Technology on Review Quality
- arXiv: 2607.13196v1
- Authors: Suzhen Zhong; Shayan Noei; Bram Adams; Ying Zou
- Full text: `data/search/provisional-extraction/supporting/ARXIV-0010.txt`
- Screening tier: supporting

## Required extraction

1. Purpose: examines how review efficiency and quality change across human-centric, LLM-assisted, and agentic review eras.
2. System/context: mining review interactions in GitHub pull requests across human, LLM, and AI-agent reviewer participation patterns.
3. Study design: 1.02 million reviewed pull requests from 207 GitHub projects; analyses reviewer interaction sequences and adoption patterns.
4. Problematic risks: reduced review quality despite efficiency gains; review activity, pull-request type, workload, and collaboration patterns are relevant explanatory factors.
5. Metrics/results: agent-involved patterns are associated with faster decisions under specified adoption patterns, but the abstract reports that these gains do not translate into better review quality.
6. Trade-off: automation and agent involvement may improve review speed while failing to improve quality; efficiency and quality must therefore be reported separately.
7. Limitations: observational repository evidence, era/adoption confounding, and indirect quality measures limit causal interpretation of AI effects. Quality score: provisional, medium.
8. Synthesis claim: supporting evidence for workflow cost, review coverage/quality separation, and socio-technical evaluation of agentic review.
