# Conclusion

This targeted structured literature review synthesized a unified corpus of 121 full-text studies relevant to trade-off-aware evaluation of LLM-based code review. Failures can originate in generated comments, context, data, workflow, agent trajectories, or evaluation procedures. These sources require different diagnoses and interventions.

Mitigation studies often report quality gains without consistently reporting or evaluating useful-feedback preservation, review or issue coverage, human workload, or computational cost. This paper proposes a taxonomy and six-layer framework for recording these outcomes and supporting decisions to show, suppress, rewrite, or escalate feedback.

The later multi-source search amendment produced a large auditable candidate pool, but these records have not undergone complete eligibility assessment and extraction. The synthesis findings therefore remain limited to the frozen 121-study corpus. Independent annotation and controlled studies remain necessary before the proposed taxonomy and framework can support empirical or deployment claims.
