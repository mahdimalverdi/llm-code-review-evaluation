# Conclusion

This targeted structured review synthesized 121 full-text studies relevant to trade-off-aware evaluation of LLM-based code review. The evidence shows that problematic feedback cannot be reduced to hallucination alone and that review quality cannot be represented by a single similarity, correctness, relevance, usefulness, acceptance, or judge score. Mitigation mechanisms also require asymmetric evaluation: reducing visible errors is insufficient unless useful-feedback preservation, review coverage, escalation, workflow effects, and cost are reported.

The review derives an operational taxonomy, separates evaluation dimensions from failure types and mitigation decisions, and proposes a framework spanning context quality, comment quality, preservation, coverage, cost, and evaluator validity. It also identifies context integrity and evaluator robustness as first-class concerns, particularly when LLM judges or security-oriented settings are involved.

The findings are bounded by the assembled corpus. Because the original database search and screening history was not preserved, the work should not yet be described as a fully reproducible SLR. A documented search rerun, independent calibration, citation and metadata verification, and empirical validation of the taxonomy are the principal next steps.
